# Lesson presentation master template v1

Derived from the uploaded P001 presentation and P001 teacher-guide patterns.

## Stable frame
- 16:9.
- College identity in the header.
- Lesson/topic context remains visible but subordinate to the slide title.
- Slide number is persistent.
- One dominant teaching purpose per slide.
- Bottom/result zone contains the action, criterion, or takeaway — not service notes.

## Semantic slide layouts
1. cover — college identity, module, lesson number, full topic, short course line, hero visual.
2. hook — why the topic matters; one professional consequence.
3. why — challenge a superficial assumption with WHY/BECAUSE structure.
4. observable-criteria — numbered criteria that can be pointed to or checked.
5. teacher-demo — action → explanation → student repeat → verification.
6. worked-example — one controlled case; evidence before conclusion.
7. boundary — what can/cannot be concluded from the evidence.
8. compare-pair — A/B with one controlled variable.
9. why-question — a single causal question.
10. answer-upgrade — everyday wording → professional wording.
11. timed-observation — 60–90 s visual search with explicit count/criteria.
12. mini-debate — choose priority and justify by evidence.
13. strong-conclusion — fact → meaning/risk → action/conclusion.
14. checklist — short pre-work verification.
15. summary — observable learning result + bridge to next lesson.

## Interaction grammar
Every interactive slide must state:
- what students inspect/do;
- time or turn-taking rule when relevant;
- what counts as evidence;
- expected form of answer.

## Teacher-guide grammar
For each major concept:
1. show the initial state without explanation;
2. allow observation;
3. change/name one condition;
4. ask for a prediction;
5. reveal/compare;
6. state the causal chain;
7. name the boundary of the conclusion;
8. transfer the criterion to a new example.

## Evidence language
Prefer: object → observable feature → consequence → professional conclusion.
Avoid unsupported taste judgements such as “better”, “beautiful”, “bad” without a task and evidence.

## Visual rule
A/B comparison images must keep all non-tested parameters as stable as possible. Visuals should vary by teaching function across the lesson, not repeat one composition.
